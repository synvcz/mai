Build a single-page scroll website titled “Character / 01 — Why Her”, centered entirely around Mai Sakurajima from Rascal Does Not Dream of Bunny Girl Senpai.

The website is a personal visual essay about why I chose Mai Sakurajima over every other fictional character.

This should not feel like an objective argument that Mai is universally the “best girl.” It should feel like my own journey of choosing her: what first caught my attention, what made me stay, what I discovered about her, and why she eventually became the character I connected with most.

The central question throughout the website is:

“Why did I choose her?”

Visual Direction

Create a sophisticated, cinematic character-study website inspired by premium product films and contemporary art direction.

Do not use fashion-editorial aesthetics. This is not a fashion campaign or magazine spread.

Alternate between soft silver-grey / ivory environments and near-black sections.

The visual language should be quiet, elegant, restrained, introspective, and emotional without becoming melodramatic.

Use enormous negative space, subtle gradients, delicate film grain, precise typography, and carefully composed anime artwork.

Mai should remain the visual center of the entire experience, but the story being told is my relationship with the character, not an objective character analysis.

The website should feel like an interactive personal film about a choice.

---

Hero — Why Her

Open with a nearly empty silver-grey environment.

Place a large cinematic portrait of Mai Sakurajima floating within the space, slightly off-center.

Keep the composition extremely minimal.

Tiny uppercase monospace metadata sits in the corners:

"CHARACTER / 01"
"MAI SAKURAJIMA"
"A PERSONAL STUDY"

Large, heavy, tightly tracked typography appears gradually:

“Why her.”

Below it:

“Out of everyone, I chose her.”

As the user begins scrolling, the image subtly moves while the typography remains almost perfectly still.

The opening should immediately establish that this is my choice, not a universal ranking.

---

Chapter 01 — The First Impression

Transition into a near-black section.

Keep Mai isolated within a huge field of negative space.

Headline:

“Something / caught my attention.”

Small caption:

I had never met Mai before. There were countless other characters I could have noticed instead. But something about her made me stop and pay attention.

It might have been her presence, her expressions, her personality, or simply the feeling she gave me when she first appeared.

The important part is that she was the one who made me want to keep watching.

Tiny corner labels:

"01 / FIRST IMPRESSION"
"THE BEGINNING"
"SOMETHING STAYED"

Let the next section slowly slide over this one.

---

Chapter 02 — What I Found

Transition back into a pale silver-grey environment.

Show a close cinematic portrait emphasizing Mai's expression.

Headline:

“Then I / got to know her.”

Caption:

The more I saw of Mai, the more I realized that what initially caught my attention wasn't the whole reason I liked her.

Her confidence, humor, sarcasm, kindness, independence, and vulnerability gradually became part of the picture.

What started as interest became attachment.

Use subtle annotations:

"02 / DISCOVERY"
"PERSONALITY"
"CHARACTER"
"CONNECTION"

Chapter 03 — Not Just Her Appearance

Move into a near-black environment.

Place Mai alone against a huge empty composition.

Headline:

“It wasn't / just how she looked.”

Caption:

Mai is beautiful, and that was certainly part of my first impression.

But appearance alone could never explain why I chose her.

What kept me interested was everything underneath it: the way she speaks, the way she thinks, the way she treats people, the way she hides vulnerability behind confidence, and the way she remains herself.

Technical labels:

"03 / BEYOND APPEARANCE"
"PERSONALITY"
"INDEPENDENCE"

Chapter 04 — The Contrast

Transition into a softer, dimly lit environment.

Use a more emotionally expressive image of Mai.

Headline:

“The more I saw, / the more there was.”

Caption:

Mai isn't just confident.

She can be vulnerable.

She isn't just sarcastic.

She can be incredibly caring.

She isn't simply independent.

She can also allow herself to rely on someone she trusts.

These contrasts are part of what made her feel like more than a collection of traits to me.

They made her feel like Mai.

Keep this section visually restrained.

No excessive effects.

No dramatic particles.

Just the image, typography, and space.

There are countless characters who are funny, beautiful, intelligent, kind, interesting, or emotionally compelling.

Typography

Use a heavy modern sans-serif for all major headlines.

Headlines should be extremely large, tightly tracked, and carefully positioned.

Supporting copy should remain small and understated.

Use a monospace uppercase font for chapter numbers, metadata, annotations, and technical captions.

Maintain a strict hierarchy:

1. Cinematic headline
2. Personal supporting paragraph
3. Tiny metadata

The writing should consistently use a personal first-person perspective where appropriate.

Do not write as though the website is proving that Mai is objectively superior to other characters.

Do not use decorative anime fonts.

---

Motion

Make scrolling feel like moving through a slow personal character film.

- Pin major artwork while the surrounding text changes
- Let each chapter slide over the previous chapter
- Use subtle parallax on artwork
- Slowly scale portraits during transitions
- Fade typography in and out
- Allow annotations to drift slightly
- Use long, smooth easing
- Create deliberate moments of visual silence
- Let important statements appear slowly rather than all at once

Avoid flashy anime effects, particle systems, excessive blur, bouncing elements, or conventional website animations.

The motion should feel reflective rather than energetic.

---

Overall Feel

The final website should feel like a personal cinematic reflection on choosing one fictional character among countless others.

Quiet.

Cinematic.

Minimal.

Personal.

Introspective.

A little melancholic.

Never cluttered.

The central idea should gradually become clear:

I didn't choose Mai because she is objectively better than every other character.

I chose her because something about her resonated with me in a way no other character did.

The entire website should gradually answer one simple question:

Why her?

And the final answer should not be a list.

It should be a choice:

“Because, out of everyone, she became the one I chose.”
